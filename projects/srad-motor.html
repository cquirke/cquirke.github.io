SRAD H-Class Solid Rocket Motor

38mm RNX-71 — Design Through Manufacturing

Propulsion SRAD MATLAB ANSYS Nozzle Design
Class 38mm H-Class / 400 psi
Nozzle 2-Part Al / Phenolic, Conical
Analysis MATLAB / ANSYS / RPA
Status Paused — Resuming Independently

Overview

This was Liberty Rocketry's first attempt at a fully student-researched-and-developed motor. I led the project end-to-end (design, analysis, and the start of manufacturing) before it was paused by the university. Development continues independently.

Why RNX-71

As a first SRAD engine, minimizing risk while proving out the full design-to-manufacture pipeline mattered more than performance. RNX-71 is inexpensive, low-power, and low-consequence, making it well suited for validating process and procedure, including navigating the university's safety approval, before scaling up.

Process

Starting from the target scope (38mm, H-class, 500 psi chamber pressure), I worked outward: 1. From performance goals and propellant charecteristics the team calculated rough geometries and motor characteristics. 2. I created a decision matrix in MATLAB based on 1. 3. Worked with a peer to finalize propellant composition and necessary thermodynamic and burn rate properties. 4. I built a target model and then modeled in BurnSim4.0 and compared against OpenMotor. 5. I modeled the design in SolidWorks, GD and T the fitment, and 3D-printed the assembly to verify. 6. Finalized manufacturing plan, drawings, and necessary equipment. Purchased items keeping motor components under $80. 7. Started manufacturing.

Motor Charecteristics

Hand calculations for design were done as well as a decsision matrix, shown below. Our parameters were as follows: * Mass flux less than 1200 kg/m^2-sec * Minimum port to throat = 1.1 * Chamber Port Mach less than 0.7 Additionaly the plot shown shows the balance between Pressure, Mass Flux, and mass flow rate.

Hardware

Designed a two-part nozzle: an aluminum carrier with a phenolic insert retained by a thread design and the insert retained by snap-ring, a singular O-ring is used on the AFT closure for hot gasses, the phenolic insert is designed with a lip over the carrier to protect against hot gasses and allow a 2 piece nozzle. The FWD closure is made of a 6061 aluminum with a phenolic disk protecting it during combustion. It is designed with 2 O-rings, one that seals gasses from going behind the nozzle liner and another for redundancy. The insulation liner is an off the shelf phenolic liner from Aerotech. Chosen for simplicity and tradeoff advantage. Materials were chosen by there physical charecteristics and considered for ease of manufacturing. Although Graphite is typical it was not chosen based on price of manufacturing, some things that allowed us to make this descision are the conservative chamber pressure and designing the phenolic insert to be single use.

Motor Performance

Final design led to: * Impulse = 274 N-s (this is 71% of the H class) * Delivered Impulse = 121 s * Burn Time 1.19 s * Average Thrust = 230 N (Peak = 321.8 N) Considerations: Given solid rocket motors of comparable size and high power propellant this is a very well performing engine. Goal was to model best performance for given restrictions, 500 psi, G less than or equal to 1.8. Comparison: BurnSim4.0 and OpenMotor More weight was put into the result of BurnSim as its physics solver is more accurate and refined. Additionally, more variabillity is allowed in BurnSim for timestep and erosive burning; the Lenoir Robillard erosive burn model was used for this motor because of its accuracy with low powered small motors. OpenMotor was used to confirm results and sensitivity.

Manufacturing & Process Planning

While the case and closure hardware was on order, I finalized the propellant mixing and casting process, including batch ratios and quality-control checks. The manufacturing plan called for 4 grains — 3 for the motor, 1 dedicated to quality testing. Case and closure manufacturing began once material arrived, before the project was paused.

Status

Design is complete and validated across multiple tools. Manufacturing is paused pending university review and will resume independently.
